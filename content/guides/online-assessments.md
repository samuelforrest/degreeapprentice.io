After you apply, most large employers send you online assessments. They're used to shortlist candidates fairly when hundreds of people apply, and they're often the stage where most applicants are filtered out. The good news is that practice makes a real difference, mainly because you get used to the formats and the time pressure.

## The common types

- **Situational judgement tests (SJTs):** workplace scenarios where you choose or rate the best responses. Almost every large apprenticeship scheme uses one. They measure judgement and the behaviours the employer values.
- **Numerical reasoning:** questions about tables and charts, involving percentages, ratios and currency conversions. You can usually use a calculator.
- **Verbal reasoning:** read a short passage and decide whether statements are true, false or can't be decided from the text.
- **Logical reasoning:** spot patterns in sequences of shapes (inductive) or work out what must follow from a set of statements (deductive).
- **Game-based assessments:** short games that measure things like attention, memory and how you handle risk. There's usually no right way to play, so just follow the instructions and stay focused.
- **Personality questionnaires:** statements you agree or disagree with. Answer honestly and consistently. Trying to guess the "right" profile usually shows up as inconsistency.

You can practise SJTs, numerical, verbal and logical reasoning on our [free practice tests](/tests).

## How to prepare

1. **Research the employer.** Read their values or behaviours page. SJTs are built around them, and knowing them helps you choose responses that fit.
2. **Practise each format under time pressure.** Speed matters as much as accuracy. Do at least one timed practice test of each type the employer uses.
3. **Review your mistakes.** Reading the explanations teaches you more than doing extra questions.
4. **Brush up on percentages.** Percentage change, percentage of a total and reverse percentages come up in nearly every numerical test.

## On the day

- **Use a laptop or computer, not your phone**, and a reliable internet connection.
- **Choose a quiet time** when you won't be interrupted. Most tests can't be paused.
- **Have a calculator and rough paper** ready for numerical tests.
- **Don't get stuck.** If a question is taking too long, make your best guess and move on. Most tests don't penalise wrong answers, so never leave one blank.
- **Do it yourself.** Asking someone else to help is cheating, and many employers retest you later.

## If you don't pass

It happens to almost everyone at some point. Most employers ask you to wait before reapplying, often until the next recruitment year. Keep practising, and apply to other schemes in the meantime. Each attempt makes the next one easier.
