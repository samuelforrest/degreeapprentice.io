You don't have to choose between university and a degree apprenticeship before you apply. Many students apply for both and decide once they know what's on offer. Apprenticeships are competitive and places are limited, so a university application is a sensible backup, and sometimes it turns out to be the better choice.

## How the two processes differ

- **University:** one UCAS application with up to five choices, one personal statement, and a main deadline at the end of January for most courses.
- **Apprenticeships:** separate applications to each employer, each with its own form, tests and interviews, and deadlines spread from autumn to summer.

The two systems don't interfere with each other. Applying to university doesn't affect your apprenticeship applications, and employers won't see your UCAS choices.

## Managing the workload

Apprenticeship applications take far more time than one UCAS form. To keep on top of them:

- **Prioritise quality over quantity.** Five well-researched applications beat twenty rushed ones.
- **Reuse your materials.** Your personal statement and apprenticeship answers can share ideas and examples, but adapt them each time. See [how to answer "why this apprenticeship?"](/guides/why-this-apprenticeship).
- **Track everything** in one place: deadlines, logins, test invitations and interview dates.
- **Protect your study time.** Your grades matter for both routes, because most offers depend on them.

## When the offers arrive

University offers usually arrive between autumn and spring, and you're asked to choose a firm and an insurance choice by a deadline, often in early summer. Apprenticeship offers can arrive at any time, sometimes after you've replied to UCAS.

That's fine. If you accept a university place and later get an apprenticeship offer you prefer, you can withdraw from university before the course starts. Do it properly through UCAS, and tell the university, so the place can go to someone else.

If you're holding offers from both, compare them on more than money: the role, the employer, the university and the qualification, where you'd live, and how each fits the career you want.

## If no apprenticeship offer comes through

It's common, because many schemes receive hundreds of applications per place. University is a great route too. Many employers also run graduate schemes and summer internships for students, and some degree apprenticeships accept people a year or two after school.
