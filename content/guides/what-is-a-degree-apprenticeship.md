A degree apprenticeship is a full-time job that includes a university degree. You're employed and paid a salary, and you study part-time for a bachelor's or master's degree. Your employer and the government pay your tuition fees, so you graduate without student loan debt for tuition.

## The two levels

- **Level 6** leads to a bachelor's degree, such as a BSc or BEng. Most degree apprenticeships are Level 6, and they usually take 3 to 5 years.
- **Level 7** leads to a master's degree. Some are open to school leavers (for example, integrated master's routes in engineering), but many are aimed at people who already have a degree or work experience.

Everything listed on [our roles page](/roles) is Level 6 or 7.

## How the week works

You spend most of your time working, and part of your paid time studying. Employers and universities organise this differently:

- **Day release:** one day a week at university or studying online.
- **Block release:** a few weeks at a time on campus, a few times a year.
- **Blended:** mostly online learning, with occasional campus days.

The time set aside for study is written into your apprenticeship agreement. It's part of your job, not something you squeeze into evenings, although most apprentices do some extra study around assessments.

## What you earn

You're paid a real salary from day one. Degree apprenticeship salaries vary widely: some employers pay close to the apprentice minimum wage, while large firms in finance, technology and engineering often pay £20,000 to £30,000 a year to start, sometimes more, and raise pay as you progress. Each listing on this site shows the salary when the employer publishes it.

## Who pays for the degree

Your tuition is funded by your employer and the government. You don't take out a tuition fee loan and you don't pay anything back. Because you're earning a salary, you also won't usually need a maintenance loan, although you'll still have living costs to cover.

## What employers look for

Entry requirements are set by each employer, so always check the listing. Common requirements are:

- A levels, or equivalents such as BTECs or T Levels, often with grades or UCAS points similar to a university course.
- GCSEs in English and maths at grade 4 or 5 or above.
- Sometimes specific subjects, such as maths or physics for engineering.

Employers also care about motivation: why this career, why their company and why an apprenticeship rather than university.

## Is it right for you?

A degree apprenticeship suits you if you're fairly sure what career you want, you'd like to start working now, and you're comfortable balancing a job with study. It's harder work than full-time university, and you'll have less free time and a smaller student social life.
