"Why do you want this apprenticeship?" appears on almost every application form and in almost every interview. It looks simple, which is exactly why weak answers stand out. Employers use it to check three things: that you understand the role, that you've researched them, and that you'd stay and finish the apprenticeship.

## A structure that works

Answer three questions, in this order:

1. **Why this career?** What draws you to the work itself, and what experience or moment made you interested?
2. **Why an apprenticeship?** Why learning while working suits you better than full-time university.
3. **Why this employer?** Something specific to them that you couldn't say about their competitors.

Aim for around 200 to 300 words on an application form, or about two minutes when spoken.

## 1. Why this career

Be specific and show evidence. "I've always loved technology" says nothing. Instead, describe something you did and what it taught you:

> When I built a website for my football club, I found the problem-solving was what I enjoyed most: working out why the fixtures page broke on phones kept me up until midnight. That's why I want to become a software engineer.

## 2. Why an apprenticeship

Talk about what the apprenticeship gives you, not what university doesn't. Good reasons include applying what you learn straight away, learning from experienced colleagues, and taking responsibility early. Avoid making money or avoiding debt your main reason. They're fine to have, but they don't show interest in the job.

## 3. Why this employer

This is where most answers are weakest. Generic praise ("a market leader with great values") could apply to anyone. Find two or three specific things:

- A project, product or client they've worked on.
- Something about how their apprenticeship is structured, such as rotations, the university or the qualification.
- Something you learned from talking to someone who works there, from an insight day or from their social media.

Then link each one to you: say why it matters to you, not just that it exists.

## Common mistakes

- **Copying the company's website back to them.** They know what it says.
- **Using one answer for every application.** Recruiters can tell. Reuse your career and apprenticeship sections, but rewrite the employer section every time.
- **Talking only about what you'll get.** Mention what you'll bring, too.
- **Getting the company or role name wrong.** Check before you submit.
