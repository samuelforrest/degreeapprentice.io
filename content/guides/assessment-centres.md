An assessment centre is usually the final stage. It's a half or full day, in person or online, where several candidates take part in a series of activities. Each one is designed to show how you'd behave at work, and you're often scored against the employer's values or behaviours.

You're not usually competing directly against the other candidates. Everyone who meets the standard can get an offer, so helping others is a good thing.

## The usual activities

- **Group exercise:** a team task, such as solving a business problem or planning a project, while assessors watch. They're looking at how you work with others, not who talks most. Listen, build on other people's ideas, bring quieter people in and help the group finish on time.
- **Presentation:** a short talk, either prepared in advance or put together on the day from a brief. Keep it simple: an opening that answers the question, two or three points, and a clear conclusion. Stick to the time limit.
- **Interview:** often a mix of motivation, strengths and "tell me about a time" questions. Prepare in the same way as for a [video interview](/guides/video-interviews).
- **Case study or written exercise:** you read some information, analyse it and write a recommendation, usually in a set time. Structure matters as much as your conclusion.
- **Role play:** you deal with a situation such as an unhappy customer or a colleague who needs help, played by an assessor. Treat it as real.

There may also be a tour, talks from current apprentices or a lunch. These usually aren't assessed, but behave professionally all day.

## How to prepare

1. **Learn the employer's values or behaviours.** Assessors usually score you against them. Think of an example that shows each one.
2. **Research the company and its industry,** including recent news. You may be asked about it.
3. **Practise presenting** to someone, and time yourself.
4. **Prepare a question or two** to ask the assessors or apprentices. It shows genuine interest.
5. **Plan the logistics.** For in-person days, plan your journey and arrive early. For online days, test your setup and have a backup, such as your phone's hotspot.

## On the day

- **Treat every activity as a fresh start.** If one goes badly, don't let it affect the next. Assessors score each one separately.
- **Show your thinking.** In group and case exercises, explain how you reached your view.
- **Be yourself.** Assessors are looking for people who'll do well on the scheme, not perfect performances.

Afterwards, ask for feedback whatever the outcome. Most employers will give some, and it's invaluable for your next application.
