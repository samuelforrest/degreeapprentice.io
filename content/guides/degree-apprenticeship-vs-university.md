Degree apprenticeships are often sold as simply better than university: a degree, a salary and no debt. For many people they are a great choice, but they involve real trade-offs that are worth thinking about before you choose.

## Where degree apprenticeships win

- **No tuition fee debt.** Your employer and the government pay your fees.
- **A salary from day one,** usually rising each year.
- **Experience.** By graduation you'll have several years of real work on your CV, which many graduates spend years catching up on.
- **A clear path.** Many apprentices stay with their employer afterwards, often in a role with more responsibility.
- **Learning that sticks.** Applying what you study at work the same week helps it make sense.

## Where university wins

- **Choice and time to decide.** You can study a subject you love without committing to a career, change course, and keep your options open.
- **Breadth of subjects.** Many subjects, such as history, languages or medicine, don't have degree apprenticeship routes, or have very few.
- **The student experience.** Living away from home, societies, long holidays and time to explore. Apprentices get some of this, but much less.
- **Lower workload.** A full-time job plus a part-time degree is demanding. You'll have around 25 days of holiday a year, not long summers.
- **Places.** There are far fewer degree apprenticeships than university places, and the best-known schemes are very competitive.

## The questions that actually decide it

1. **Do you know roughly what career you want?** If so, an apprenticeship gets you there faster. If not, university gives you time to find out.
2. **Does your career have a degree apprenticeship route?** Check what's [available in your sector](/roles).
3. **How do you learn best?** Some people thrive applying things immediately; others prefer time to study in depth.
4. **What do you want from the next few years outside work?**
