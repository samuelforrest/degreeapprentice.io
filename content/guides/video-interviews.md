Many employers use a video interview after the online tests. There are two kinds:

- **Recorded (one-way) interviews:** questions appear on screen, and you record your answers alone. You usually get 30 seconds to a few minutes to think, then a fixed time to answer. Someone at the company watches the recordings later.
- **Live interviews:** a normal interview with a real person over Teams, Zoom or a similar app.

Recorded interviews feel strange at first, because there's nobody to react to you. Practice is the best fix.

## What you'll be asked

Expect a mix of:

- **Motivation questions:** "Why this apprenticeship?" and "Why our company?" See [how to answer them](/guides/why-this-apprenticeship).
- **Strength questions:** "What do you enjoy doing?" or "What are you good at?" These have no right answer. Be honest and enthusiastic.
- **Behaviour questions:** "Tell me about a time you worked in a team" or "Describe a time you solved a problem".
- **Questions about the company or industry:** recent news, products or challenges.

## Use the STAR method for "tell me about a time" questions

- **Situation:** set the scene in a sentence or two.
- **Task:** what you needed to achieve.
- **Action:** what *you* did. This should be the longest part, so say "I", not "we".
- **Result:** what happened, and what you learned.

Prepare five or six examples from school, work, hobbies or volunteering that you can adapt to different questions.

## Setting up

- **Camera at eye level,** with your face clearly lit from the front. A window in front of you works well; one behind you doesn't.
- **A plain, tidy background** and a quiet room. Tell people at home when you're recording.
- **Test your setup** with the practice question most platforms offer. Check your sound and how you look on camera.
- **Dress as you would for an in-person interview**, at least from the waist up.

## While you're answering

- **Look at the camera, not the screen**, at least some of the time. It feels like eye contact to the viewer.
- **Use the thinking time** to plan a structure, not to write a script. Reading notes is obvious on camera.
- **Keep to time.** Aim to finish with a few seconds to spare, rather than being cut off mid-sentence.
- **Smile and show energy.** Video flattens enthusiasm, so be slightly more animated than feels natural.

If something goes wrong technically, contact the employer straight away. Most will let you try again.
